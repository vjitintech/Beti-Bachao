About Beti Bachao

A free women's safety app that brings help from the people closest to her: verified volunteers nearby, her family and the police.

How the idea began

Beti Bachao started with a conversation at home. Vijay Surya and his brother-in-law, Z. Vinod Kumar, were talking about a question many families ask today: how do we keep the people we love safe?

As the conversation went on, one thought stayed with Vijay: why can't there be one app that a woman can rely on when she feels unsafe, one that does not stop at "message sent" but makes sure someone actually comes?

That question became the idea, and the idea became the app you see today.

What Beti Bachao does

  • Many ways to raise an SOS. Hold the SOS button, shake the phone, press the power button five times, say a chosen SOS word, blink at the camera, or double-press a small Bluetooth keychain button.
  • Verified volunteers. Every volunteer is checked by our team on a live video call before they can respond. Nearby volunteers are alerted in widening circles until someone accepts.
  • Family and police, together. Her emergency contacts get an SMS with her live location, and she can add her nearest police station as one of those contacts.
  • Live tracking with arrival times. She can see who is coming, how far away they are, and roughly when they will arrive.
  • Safety until she says so. An SOS ends only when she confirms she is safe. A duress PIN looks like "cancel" but quietly keeps help coming.
  • Built for everyone. Spoken guidance and screen-reader support for blind and low-vision women, in 14 languages.
